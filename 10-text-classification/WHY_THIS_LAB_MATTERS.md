## 🔬 WHAT DOES THIS LAB COVER?

### Stage 1: Text preprocessing
**Why:** Text needs to be prepared before it can be used for ML
- **Tokenization** — splitting into words
- **Lemmatization** — reducing to a dictionary form ("products" → "product")
- **Stop-word removal** — dropping "the", "a", "is" (carry little meaning)
- **Cleanup** — removing special characters, extra whitespace

**Analogy:** Prepping ingredients before cooking a dish

---

### Stage 2: Turning text into numbers
**Why:** Computers don't understand words, only numbers
- **Bag of Words** — counting word frequency
- **TF-IDF** — weighting words by importance within a document

**Analogy:** Translating text into a language the computer understands

---

### Stage 3: Training the models
**Why:** The model learns to tell classes apart (positive/negative)
- **Naive Bayes** — a probabilistic approach
- **SVM** — support vector machines
- **Random Forest** — an ensemble of decision trees

**Analogy:** Teaching a child to tell "good" from "bad" from examples

---

### Stage 4: Evaluating quality
**Why:** We need to know how well the model actually performs
- **Accuracy** — overall correctness
- **Precision** — correctness of predictions
- **Recall** — coverage (how many were found)
- **F1-score** — the balance between precision and recall

**Analogy:** An exam that tests the model's knowledge

---

### Stage 5: Applying it to new data
**Why:** Using the trained model on real tasks
- Scraping new reviews from websites
- Classifying them automatically
- Reading off the results

**Analogy:** Putting the knowledge into practice
